### 💡 GuitarSetupCheck AI: Microphone Fret-Buzz & Camera Action-Height Setup Diagnostician

**Context Category**: Mobile App (iOS & Android) — *Category G: Hardware/Sensor (Audio + Camera)*
**Novelty Level**: Novel Combination, not Unprecedented (see prior art below) — mechanism overlaps with Entry #11 (FretPulse AI) already in this project's log.

#### 1. Core Problem & Latent Friction
- **Discovered Friction**: Fret buzz, wrong action height, and truss-rod issues are common electric-guitar complaints; the entrenched DIY fix is a $10–30 physical string-action gauge/ruler plus free YouTube/forum tutorials, or paying a shop $70–$200+ for a professional setup.
- **Target Audience**: Hobbyist/intermediate electric guitarists who want to self-diagnose before paying a luthier.

#### 2. The "Why Now?" Factor (Tech Enabler)
- **Recent Tech Breakthrough**: On-device audio FFT and mobile computer vision are mature and accessible via off-the-shelf SDKs.
- **Reality Check**: Composing an FFT pitch-detection SDK is mature and off-the-shelf. Classifying *buzz specifically* (a rattle transient distinct from pick noise, string squeak, amp/room reverb, and player-technique buzz) is **not** an off-the-shelf capability — no pretrained model or public dataset for this task was found (see Evidence Log). The "why now" argument is weaker than usual: general audio ML is mature, but the *specific* classifier this app needs still has to be invented from scratch.

#### 3. Novelty & Prior-Art Verification
- **Prior-Art Search Results**: Real, materially relevant prior art exists. It is a *novel combination/flank*, not an unprecedented concept.
- **Originality Verdict**: Flanking Existing Competitors — not unprecedented.

#### 4. Anti-False-Positive 7-Proof Verification Matrix

- **Proof 1 (Willingness to Pay & Demand Velocity)**: **PASS (weak)**. Real money is spent on the underlying problem — $70–$200+ luthier setups, $10–30 physical gauges, and a shipped app (Suede) already bundles a "fret buzz vs. action" diagnostic feature. However, no evidence of rising search/trend momentum was found, and no Upwork/Fiverr job posts or reviews proving people pay *specifically* for a standalone diagnostic app were located — the strongest evidence is for paying to *fix* the problem (labor), not to *diagnose* it via software.
- **Proof 2 (Zero-CAC Distribution)**: **PASS**. Large, virality-friendly guitar-gear TikTok/YouTube niche and active communities (r/guitar, r/Luthier) are a genuine organic channel.
- **Proof 3 (Anti-Churn Retention)**: **FAIL**. A setup diagnosis is not a daily/weekly task. Once a guitar's action/relief is dialed in, it holds for months; re-checks happen only after string changes, seasonal humidity swings, or when something starts sounding wrong — realistically a quarterly-or-rarer event per guitar. This is structurally the same rare-event churn risk the log already penalized elsewhere (Entry: CollisionProof AI was downgraded to PIVOT REQUIRED for exactly this reason and required adding a daily-use mode to pass Proof 3).
- **Proof 4 (AI Reliability >95%)**: **FAIL**. No pretrained model or public dataset for fret-buzz classification exists (confirmed via search); the closest published buzz-classification research (bat/insect bioacoustics) reports 93–95% in unrelated domains, not guitar. Buzz has multiple confounding causes (setup issue vs. player technique vs. string/pickup noise) that even human players struggle to distinguish by ear alone per community guidance found in search results. A wrong truss-rod turn-direction recommendation carries real downside (neck damage), making this a high-stakes edge case, not a "minor inconvenience" failure mode.
- **Proof 5 (Micro-Moat)**: **PASS (conditional)**. If the classifier and camera-calibration pipeline actually work reliably, the tuned heuristics/calibration data would be a genuine moat not trivially cloned. This is conditional on Proof 4/7 being solved first.
- **Proof 6 (Status Quo Resistance)**: **FAIL**. The existing non-software substitute — a $10–30 physical action gauge plus free tutorials — is cheap, one-time, and by community consensus *more* accurate for sub-millimeter tolerances than a camera-only measurement without a physical reference object. The app does not clearly save 2+ hours/week (the task itself is infrequent) or beat the $200+/month bar; it's competing against an already-good-enough $15 one-time tool.
- **Proof 7 (True Solopreneur Buildability)**: **FAIL**. This is the crux of the user's question. Both core mechanisms carry every Proof-7 red flag: (a) custom FFT-based classification of a novel signal (buzz transient vs. harmonic pluck vs. ambient noise) with no existing model/dataset to build on, requiring the developer to invent and empirically tune the classifier against many real guitars, pickups, string types, and rooms; (b) camera-based sub-millimeter distance estimation, which per the evidence above is trusted by the community only when paired with a physical reference/calibration object — not a claim the original "5-7 days" estimate accounted for. Neither has a graceful, cheap fallback that still delivers the core value if the novel component underperforms (an app that can't reliably tell buzz from no-buzz, or that mis-measures action height, isn't just "less polished" — it gives actively wrong repair advice).
  - **Realistic build-time breakdown**: Core CRUD/UI (guitar profiles, capture flow, results screens) — **~1 week**, in line with the log's simple-app baseline. Novel component (buzz classifier design + calibration against real guitars/phones + camera height estimation with a reference-object UX + truss-rod-direction heuristics validated for safety) — **an additional 5-9 weeks** of iterative real-hardware testing, not a few days, because accuracy has to be earned empirically, not assumed. **Total realistic solo MVP: roughly 6-10 weeks (1.5-2.5 months)** for a version honest enough to publish an accuracy claim — not the "5-7 days" figure already logged for this same mechanism under Entry #11.
